\chapter{Does the surface hold?}\label{chapter:validation}
 
\section{Introduction}
 
The national map left one question open: whether a relationship estimated
between cities holds at the finer scale inside them. The age split left
another: whether the gradient it read off the national pooling survives at
the scale of the single neighbourhood. 
 
To validate a fitted curve, one holds out part of the data, predicts it from
the rest, and checks the prediction against what was held back. But the
surface here is predicted, not fitted: no neighbourhood carries enough deaths
to fit a curve to its own mortality, which is the whole reason the risk is
read off the national model instead.
 
Two tests remain. The first plants a known structure in simulated mortality
and asks whether the pipeline recovers it---the register on which the case
time series design was itself validated~\citep{gasparrini2021}. The second
takes the surface fitted to real deaths and asks whether it agrees with what
is known from outside the model: whether, for example, the vulnerability
slope runs in the established direction, the minimum-mortality temperature
tracks climate, and the map agrees with heat events already on record. The
first tests the pipeline where the answer is planted; the second tests its
output against a world it did not fit to. This chapter takes them in turn 
and reports where each holds and where it does not.
